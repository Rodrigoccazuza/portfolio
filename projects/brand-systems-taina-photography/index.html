04, Guidelines

System details

01

Visual system

The website extends the warm palette into an NYC-inspired visual language: dark asphalt-like surfaces, cream paper tones, taxi-yellow highlights, brick warmth, and restrained editorial spacing. Strong typography supports the photography instead of competing with it.

02

Photography direction

Prioritize natural expressions, real interactions, environmental context, and recognizable New York atmosphere. The work should feel observed rather than over-produced, with room for street texture, Central Park, travel moments, proposals, families, and golden-hour beach sessions.

03

Type hierarchy

Poppins carries the bold visual personality, Inter keeps longer information calm and readable, and JetBrains Mono adds a compact editorial voice for metadata and labels. The contrast creates energy without turning the site into a fashion editorial that overwhelms the images.

04

Voice & messaging

The live website positions the service as a photography experience rather than a formal studio appointment. The voice is warm and welcoming, but also clear about who the sessions are for, where they happen, and what clients can book.

  • Warm
  • Relaxed
  • Natural
  • Local

Messaging principles

  • Describe the feeling of the experience before over-explaining the technical process.
  • Use simple, human language for couples, families, solo travelers, proposals, and vacation sessions.
  • Keep New York present in the story: locations and atmosphere are part of the product.
  • Name packages like experiences, not commodities, so the offer feels memorable and personal.
  • Keep booking language reassuring and practical, with clear session types, pricing, and next steps.
05

Service naming

The website uses experience-led names such as NYC Mini Experience, The Complete New York Experience, and Golden Hour by the Ocean. That naming system should carry into social posts, booking materials, and future packages.
